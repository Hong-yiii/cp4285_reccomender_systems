## Distinctions the shared implementation must protect

1. **Sequence order is not update order.** A valid user history may be an input to an interleaved training schedule. Mix complete examples/batches, never splice unrelated users' histories.
2. **A proxy is not ideal ground truth.** Amazon review events are not purchase timestamps or proof of the best recommendation. A five-star target was mentioned, but the meeting did not settle a change from the earlier all-rating trial.
3. **Retention and adaptation are different outcomes.** Hold old queries, targets and candidates fixed; also measure the new task. A frozen learner can retain old performance simply by refusing to learn anything.
4. **Mixtures need a denominator and budget.** Recommended boilerplate convention: B supervised targets / all supervised targets. Fixed total targets means a larger B fraction reduces A exposure. This alone does not identify harmful B gradients.
5. **Capacity needs a definition.** State whether width, depth or embeddings change; include catalogue-dependent embeddings in total/trainable parameter counts. Equal examples, equal optimizer steps and equal compute are different comparisons.
6. **A lower score is not proof that a particular weight or memory was erased.** Describe observed ranking changes under the stated protocol. Informal transfer definitions and claims about external papers still need primary-source verification.
